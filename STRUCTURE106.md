## 106A. Time Series, Stochastic Processes, and Spatial Data

None of the three libraries defines a weakly stationary process, an autocovariance function, or a spectral density of a process. The recorded material is the \(L^2\) and Gaussian infrastructure from which such a theory would be built: covariance and variance of finite sums, orthogonal projection and Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, Gaussian conditioning, positive-definite functions on \(\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb R^d\), the mean ergodic theorem, and the path law of a Markov chain. The general probability, martingale, and Markov-chain foundations are described in [CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md) and [CKMA Structure 08](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE08.md).

Items 106.1–106.11 precede the first topic heading of the synopsis and are inventoried here.

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#synopsis-106a).

#### Mathlib

**Limit theorems (supporting, item 1).** The only central limit theorem is the i.i.d. one in dimension one: if the \(X_k\) are independent, identically distributed, and square-integrable, then \(n^{-1/2}\sum_{k<n}(X_k-\mathbb EX_0)\) converges in distribution to the centered Gaussian of variance \(\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}X_0\) (`tendstoInDistribution_inv_sqrt_mul_sum_sub`, `Probability/CentralLimitTheorem.lean`). There is no triangular-array, Lindeberg, or martingale-difference version. Lévy's continuity theorem, the Cramér–Wold device, and Slutsky's lemma, which a proof of item 1 by characteristic functions would use, are recorded in [CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md#characteristic-functions-and-classical-distributional-limits). Discrete-time martingales, Doob's decomposition, and conditional expectation are recorded in [CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md#discrete-time-martingales), and the Azuma–Hoeffding inequality in [CKMA Structure 07](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE07.md).

**Hilbert-space prediction (supporting, items 2, 7).** The orthogonal projection onto a subspace with an orthogonal projection, in particular onto a complete subspace, minimizes the distance: \(\|y-Py\|=\inf_{x\in U}\|y-x\|\) (`Submodule.starProjection_minimal`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Basic.lean`), and the minimizer exists for every complete subspace (`Submodule.exists_norm_eq_iInf_of_complete_subspace`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Minimal.lean`). Applied in \(L^2(\mathbb P)\) to the closed linear span of past values this is the best linear predictor \(P_{t-1}X_t\) of the synopsis, but the innovation sequence, the remote past, and the Wold decomposition are not formed. Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization of a family indexed by a well-founded locally finite linear order produces pairwise orthogonal vectors with the same initial spans (`gramSchmidt_orthogonal`, `mem_span_gramSchmidt`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/GramSchmidtOrtho.lean`); in \(L^2\) these are the finite-sample innovations \(X_t-\hat X_t\).

**Multivariate Gaussian laws (supporting, items 3, 5, 7).** The multivariate Gaussian on \(\mathbb R^\iota\) with given mean and positive semidefinite covariance matrix is `multivariateGaussian` (`Probability/Distributions/Gaussian/Multivariate.lean`); its affine images and the independence of jointly Gaussian components with zero cross-covariance are recorded in [CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md#gaussian-laws-infinite-divisibility-and-exchangeability). Schur-complement identities for block matrices are in `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean`. No Fisher information is defined.

**Markov kernels (supporting, items 8, 19).** Kernels, their compositions, invariant measures, reversibility, \(\varphi\)-irreducibility, and the Ionescu–Tulcea construction of trajectory laws are recorded in [CKMA Structure 08](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE08.md#markov-semigroups-martingale-problems-and-chains) (`Probability/Kernel/Invariance.lean`, `Probability/Kernel/Irreducible.lean`, `Probability/Kernel/IonescuTulcea/Traj.lean`).

**Stationarity and ergodic averages (supporting, items 9, 11).** Von Neumann's mean ergodic theorem: for a continuous linear map of norm at most one on a real or complex Hilbert space, the Birkhoff averages converge strongly to the orthogonal projection onto the fixed space (`ContinuousLinearMap.tendsto_birkhoffAverage_orthogonalProjection`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/MeanErgodic.lean`). For a weakly stationary sequence the shift on the closed span of the \(X_t\) is unitary, so this gives \(L^2\) convergence of \(\bar X_n\); this specialization is not stated. The maximal ergodic theorem for a measure-preserving map is proved: the integral of an integrable \(g\) over the set where some Birkhoff sum is positive is nonnegative, and the maximal ergodic operator satisfies the weak-type \((1,1)\) inequality (`setIntegral_birkhoffSumSup_nonneg`, `mul_measureReal_lt_birkhoffAverageSup_le_integral`, `Dynamics/BirkhoffSum/Maximal.lean`); Birkhoff's pointwise ergodic theorem is not derived from it. Projective families of finite measures on products of Polish spaces have a projective limit (`MeasureTheory.projectiveLimit`, `isProjectiveLimit_projectiveLimit`, `MeasureTheory/Constructions/KolmogorovExtension.lean`), the measure-theoretic input for constructing a process from its finite-dimensional laws. Strong mixing coefficients, long-memory asymptotics, and resampling are absent.

#### TauCeti

**Gaussian conditioning (item 7).** For a random vector with law \(N(m,S)\) on \(\mathbb R^{\iota\sqcup\kappa}\), with \(S\) positive semidefinite, the regular conditional distribution of the first block given the second is, almost everywhere, the Gaussian kernel with mean \(m_1+S_{12}S_{22}^{+}(x_2-m_2)\) and covariance \(S_{11}-S_{12}S_{22}^{+}S_{21}\), where \(S_{22}^{+}\) is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the observed block ([Gaussian conditioning](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Distributions/Gaussian/Conditional/Basic.lean), `TauCeti.Probability.condDistrib_multivariateGaussian`, with the parameters `EuclideanSpace.gaussianCondMean` and `Matrix.gaussianCondCov`; `Matrix.covariancePseudoInverse`, [pseudoinverse](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/CovariancePseudoInverse.lean)). When \(S_{22}\) is positive definite, \(S_{22}^{+}=S_{22}^{-1}\) and the parameters take their classical form (`EuclideanSpace.gaussianCondMean_of_posDef`, `Matrix.gaussianCondCov_of_posDef`). For \(t=1\), taking the first block to be \(x_1\) and the second to be \(y_1=H_1x_1+v_1\), the joint covariance has \(S_{22}=\Sigma_1\), \(S_{12}=P_{1\mid0}H_1^\top\), and the theorem gives the first measurement update of item 7: mean \(x_{1\mid0}+K_1(y_1-H_1x_{1\mid0})\) and covariance \(P_{1\mid0}-K_1H_1P_{1\mid0}\). This specialization is not stated, and the recursion in \(t\), which requires conditioning on a growing observation vector, is not developed.

**Markov chain path laws (supporting, items 8, 19).** The path law of a time-homogeneous Markov chain with initial law \(\nu\) and transition kernel \(\kappa\) is constructed on \(\alpha^{\mathbb N}\), with the Markov property and, on a space with measurable singletons, the cylinder formula \(\mathbb P(X_0=w_0,\ldots,X_n=w_n)=\nu\{w_0\}\prod_{i<n}\kappa(w_i)\{w_{i+1}\}\) ([Markov chains](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Process/MarkovChain.lean), `TauCeti.Probability.markovChainLaw`, `TauCeti.Probability.markovChainLaw_map_prefix_succ_eq_compProd`, `TauCeti.Probability.markovChainLaw_map_prefix_apply_singleton`). This is the likelihood of an observed finite chain. A chain is also realized from independent noise driving measurable update maps ([simulation](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/MarkovChain/Simulation.lean)), which is the form in which an emission kernel would be attached. Hidden states, emission densities, and the forward recursion of item 8 are not formed.

**Ergodic averages (supporting, items 9, 11, 12).** For a measure-preserving map \(T\) with \(\sigma\)-finite trimmed measure on the invariant \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\mathcal I\), and \(f\) integrable and square-integrable, the Birkhoff averages converge in \(L^2\) to \(\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal I]\) ([mean ergodic theorem for conditional expectations](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Ergodic/CondExpProjection.lean), `TauCeti.Probability.tendsto_eLpNorm_birkhoffAverage_sub_condExp`). Applied to the shift on the path space of a strictly stationary sequence this is the \(L^2\) law of large numbers for the sample mean. Further ergodic material is in [CKMA Structure 10](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE10.md).

### Mean estimation and innovation likelihoods

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#mean-estimation-and-innovation-likelihoods).

#### Mathlib

The variance of a finite sum of square-integrable real random variables is the double sum of covariances, \(\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}\sum_{i\in s}X_i=\sum_{i,j\in s}\mathop{\mathrm{Cov}}(X_i,X_j)\) (`variance_sum'`, `variance_sum`, `Probability/Moments/Variance.lean`), and covariance is bilinear over finite sums (`covariance_sum_sum`, `Probability/Moments/Covariance.lean`). For a stationary sequence this gives \(n\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}\bar X_n=\sum_{|h|<n}(1-|h|/n)\gamma(h)\) after reindexing, which is not stated; the limit \(\sum_h\gamma(h)\) then follows by dominated convergence under absolute summability. The central limit theorem for linear processes is absent. The Gaussian density on \(\mathbb R\) is in `Probability/Distributions/Gaussian/Real.lean`.

#### TauCeti

The conditional law of \(X_t\) given \(X_1,\ldots,X_{t-1}\), for a nondegenerate Gaussian vector, is Gaussian with affine mean \(\hat X_t\) and Schur-complement variance \(v_t\), by `TauCeti.Probability.condDistrib_multivariateGaussian` above. The independence of the innovations and the factorization of the joint density into the one-step conditional densities are not stated. For independent samples, the mean-square error of a sample mean is \(r^{-2}\sum_k\mathbb E\|X_k-m\|^2\) under pairwise independence ([sample means](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/SampleMean.lean), `TauCeti.integral_norm_finiteMean_sub_sq`); this is the independent case of item 12.

### Spectral representation and linear prediction

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#spectral-representation-and-linear-prediction).

#### Mathlib

Fourier series on the circle: the Fourier coefficients of an \(L^2\) function form an orthonormal expansion, and Parseval's identity holds (`hasSum_fourier_series_L2`, `tsum_sq_fourierCoeff`, `Analysis/Fourier/AddCircle.lean`). This is the setting for a spectral density with square-summable autocovariances; no spectral measure of a process is constructed. The Gram matrix of vectors in an inner-product space is Hermitian and positive semidefinite (`Matrix.gram`, `Matrix.posSemidef_gram`, `Analysis/InnerProductSpace/GramMatrix.lean`), so an autocovariance function is nonnegative definite; the Toeplitz structure is not formed. The Durbin–Levinson recursion and Kolmogorov's formula are absent; the Szegő infimum theorem is recorded as not covered in [CKMA Structure 42](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE42.md).

#### TauCeti

Herglotz's theorem: a function \(\varphi:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C\) is positive definite, \(\sum_{i,j}c_i\bar c_j\varphi(n_i-n_j)\ge0\) for every finite family, if and only if \(\varphi(n)=\int_{\mathbb T}z^n\,d\mu(z)\) for a finite positive measure \(\mu\) on the unit circle ([Herglotz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/Herglotz.lean), `TauCeti.exists_isFiniteMeasure_integral_zpow_eq`, `TauCeti.isPositiveDefiniteSub_iff_exists_isFiniteMeasure_integral_zpow_eq`). The proof is by Fejér means and Prokhorov's theorem on the circle. The same statement for an arbitrary discrete abelian group, with a measure on the Pontryagin dual, is `TauCeti.isPositiveDefiniteSub_iff_exists_pontryaginMeasureTransform_eq` ([discrete groups](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/DiscreteGroup.lean)). The Herglotz file does not state uniqueness of \(\mu\); it follows from injectivity of the Fourier–Stieltjes transform on finite measures ([CKMA Structure 28](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE28.md)). The positive-definiteness condition is on \(\varphi(n_i-n_j)\) with complex coefficients, which contains the Hermitian property. A positive-definite kernel is realized as a Gram kernel \(K(a,b)=\langle\Phi(a),\Phi(b)\rangle\) in a minimal Hilbert space ([Kolmogorov decomposition](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/PositiveDefinite/Kernel/Kolmogorov.lean), `Matrix.PosSemidef.kolmogorovFeature`, `Matrix.PosSemidef.inner_kolmogorovFeature`); this is the Hilbert-space half of "every nonnegative definite function is an autocovariance", without a probability space. Hardy-space Toeplitz operators are developed analytically ([Toeplitz](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Toeplitz.lean)); they are not connected to prediction.

### Covariance models for random fields

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#covariance-models-for-random-fields).

#### Mathlib

#### TauCeti

Bochner's theorem on a finite-dimensional real inner-product space \(V\), in particular on \(\mathbb R^d\): a function \(F:V\to\mathbb C\) is continuous and positive definite if and only if \(F(v)=\int e^{-2\pi i\langle v,q\rangle}\,d\mu(q)\) for a unique finite positive Borel measure \(\mu\) ([Bochner](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Analysis/Bochner/BochnerTheorem.lean), `TauCeti.bochner`, `TauCeti.bochner_euclideanSpace`, `TauCeti.bochner_probabilityMeasure`; the transform uses the Fourier convention `fourierAtom`). The equivalence with being the autocovariance of a mean-square continuous field is not stated. The Cauchy law has characteristic function \(e^{ix_0t-\gamma|t|}\) ([Cauchy](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Distributions/Cauchy/Basic.lean), `TauCeti.Probability.charFun_cauchyMeasure`), so the exponential covariance \(e^{-\alpha|x|}\), the Matérn case \(\nu=1/2\), is a characteristic function on \(\mathbb R\) with Cauchy spectral measure; the Gaussian covariance is likewise the characteristic function of a Gaussian law. The general Matérn family, the moment criterion for mean-square differentiability, and conditionally negative definite functions are absent.

### Likelihood and second-order structure of point patterns

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#likelihood-and-second-order-structure-of-point-patterns).

None of the three libraries defines point processes in space, product densities, Palm distributions, or Campbell's formula. ## 106B. Survival Analysis, Sequential Analysis, and Extreme Values

The recorded material in this subdivision is mostly distributional: the hazard representation of a survival function and the additivity of hazards under independent minima, laws of the maximum and minimum of independent variables, the generalized extreme-value and generalized Pareto families with their max- and threshold-stability, the Gumbel, Weibull, Pareto, and exponential laws, renewal counting processes, Doob's maximal inequality, and the binomial-to-Poisson limit, from Mathlib and TauCeti. Statlib adds the sequential-sampling layer: Wald's identity for i.i.d. sequences, likelihood ratios and Kullback–Leibler divergences at stopping times, and e-variables. There is no counting-process martingale theory, no sequential test, and no extreme-value limit theory.

### Survival analysis and counting processes

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#survival-analysis-and-counting-processes).

#### Mathlib

#### TauCeti

For a probability measure on \(\mathbb R\) with distribution function \(F\) and survival \(S=1-F\), if \(F\) is absolutely continuous on the interval between \(a\) and \(x\), \(S>0\) there, and \(F'=f\) almost everywhere, then \(S(x)=S(a)\exp\bigl(-\int_a^xf/S\bigr)\); for a law carried by \([0,\infty)\) that is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, \(S(x)=\exp(-\int_0^x\lambda)\) whenever \(S(x)>0\), with hazard \(\lambda=f/S\), and the cumulative hazard \(-\log S\) has derivative \(\lambda\) where \(F\) is differentiable ([hazard rates](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Hazard.lean), `TauCeti.Probability.one_sub_cdf_eq_mul_exp_neg_integral`, `TauCeti.Probability.one_sub_cdf_eq_exp_neg_integral_rnDeriv`, `TauCeti.Probability.hasDerivAt_neg_log_one_sub_cdf`). ### Sequential analysis, change detection, and anytime-valid inference

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#sequential-analysis-change-detection-and-anytime-valid-inference).

#### Mathlib

Doob's maximal inequality: for a nonnegative submartingale \((f_k)\) on a finite measure space and \(\varepsilon\ge0\), \(\varepsilon\,\mu\{\max_{k\le n}f_k\ge\varepsilon\}\le\int_{\{\max_{k\le n}f_k\ge\varepsilon\}}f_n\,d\mu\) (`maximal_ineq`, `Probability/Martingale/OptionalStopping.lean`). A nonnegative martingale is a nonnegative submartingale, so for a nonnegative martingale with \(\mathbb EM_0\le1\) this gives \(\mathbb P(\max_{k\le n}M_k\ge1/\alpha)\le\alpha\) for every \(n\), and Ville's bound for the supremum over all \(n\) follows by monotone continuity of measure; neither step is stated. The supermartingale form, which is what e-processes require, is not derived; for a supermartingale the inequality would follow from optional stopping at the bounded hitting time \(\min(n,\tau_{1/\alpha})\), which Mathlib provides for submartingales and so, by negation, for supermartingales (`Probability/Martingale/OptionalStopping.lean`, `Probability/Process/HittingTime.lean`). That a product of conditionally mean-at-most-one factors is a supermartingale is not stated. The Kullback–Leibler divergence `klDiv` (`InformationTheory/KullbackLeibler/Basic.lean`) supports the quantities \(\mathbb E_i\log(f_1/f_0)\). Sequential tests, Wald's identity, and the Brownian bridge are absent; Donsker's invariance principle is not proved ([CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md#functional-limits-and-empirical-distributions)).

#### TauCeti

Doob's maximal inequality is transferred to a general linearly ordered time set: for a nonnegative submartingale on a finite measure space, \(\varepsilon\,\mathbb P(\exists t\in F,\ f_t\ge\varepsilon)\le\mathbb E f_T\) for every finite set \(F\) of times at most \(T\), and over all \(t\le T\) for right-continuous paths in separable densely ordered time ([maximal inequality](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/afa08a3ed16fb167e35de371afb1eb03fbc5913a/TauCeti/Probability/Martingale/MaximalInequality.lean), `MeasureTheory.Submartingale.mul_measure_exists_mem_le_le`, `MeasureTheory.Submartingale.mul_measure_exists_le_lt_le`). This is again the submartingale form; the supermartingale form and Ville's inequality are not stated. The other martingale files concern reverse martingales, upcrossings, convergence, and Lévy's downward theorem ([CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md#discrete-time-martingales)).

#### Statlib

**E-variables.** An e-variable for a set \(S\) of measures is a measurable \([0,\infty]\)-valued function with \(\int(X-1)\,d\mu\le0\) for every \(\mu\in S\), which for probability measures is \(\mathbb E_\mu X\le1\) (`ProbabilityTheory.IsEVar`, [e-variables](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/EValues/EVariable.lean)); the class is convex and closed under \(\liminf\) (`ProbabilityTheory.convex_isEVar`, `ProbabilityTheory.isEVar_liminf`). The product of e-variables for \(S\) and \(T\) is an e-variable for the product measures \(\mu\otimes\nu\), \(\mu\in S\), \(\nu\in T\), for finite measures (`ProbabilityTheory.IsEVar.prod`, [products](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/EValues/Product.lean)). This is the independent-product case of the e-value construction of item 38; conditional e-values, the supermartingale property of running products, optional stopping, and Ville's inequality are not stated. The numeraire e-variable of Larsson, Ramdas and Ruf exists for every finite measure and is almost surely unique (`ProbabilityTheory.exists_isNumeraire`, [numeraire existence](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/EValues/NumeraireExistence.lean); [numeraire](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/EValues/Numeraire.lean)).

**Likelihood ratios at stopping times.** For an i.i.d. sequence with law \(\mu\) or \(\nu\) and a \(\mathbb N\)-valued stopping time \(\tau\) of the coordinate filtration, the restriction of \(\mu^{\mathbb N}\) to the \(\sigma\)-algebra of \(\tau\) has density \(\prod_{i\le\tau}d\mu/d\nu(\omega_i)\) with respect to that of \(\nu^{\mathbb N}\) when \(\mu\ll\nu\), and its log-likelihood ratio is \(\sum_{i\le\tau}\log(d\mu/d\nu)(\omega_i)\) almost surely (`ProbabilityTheory.trim_stoppingTime_infinitePi_eq_withDensity`, `ProbabilityTheory.llr_trim_stoppingTime_infinitePi_ae_eq`, [sequential likelihood ratios](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/Testing/Sequential/Basic.lean)). The Kullback–Leibler divergence of the stopped laws is \(\mathbb E_\mu[\tau+1]\,\mathrm{KL}(\mu,\nu)\), in \([0,\infty]\) (`ProbabilityTheory.klDiv_trim_stoppingTime`, [stopped divergence](https://github.com/stat-lib/statlib/blob/eeaac0283690efaa60c31115400cf561836baeb9/Statlib/Testing/Sequential/KLDiv.lean)); in Statlib's indexing the observations available at \(\tau\) are \(\omega_0,\dots,\omega_\tau\), so \(\tau+1\) is the sample size \(N\) of item 37. With the data-processing inequality, this is the standard ingredient of the lower bounds for \(\mathbb E_iN\) in the Wald–Wolfowitz optimality; the sequential probability ratio test, its error bounds, and its optimality are not stated. Wald's identity is recorded under the next heading.

**Synopsis entries.** Partly covered: 106.38 (Doob's maximal inequality for nonnegative submartingales, in Mathlib, gives Ville's bound for nonnegative martingales on each finite horizon; the supermartingale case, the supremum over an infinite horizon, and the e-value consequences are not stated). Not covered: 106.37 (ingredients only: the likelihood ratio and Kullback–Leibler divergence at a stopping time and Wald's identity for i.i.d. sequences, in Statlib; finiteness of \(\mathbb E_iN\), the error bounds, and the Wald–Wolfowitz optimality are absent), 106.39, 106.40 (ingredients only: the finite-horizon maximal inequality of Mathlib and the e-variable definition of Statlib; Ville's inequality for supermartingales is absent).

### Exceedances and block maxima

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS106.md#exceedances-and-block-maxima).

#### Mathlib

If \(n\,a_n\to t\) for real \(a_n\), then \((1+a_n)^n\to e^t\) (`Real.tendsto_one_add_pow_exp_of_tendsto`, `Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Complex/LogBounds.lean`). The Poisson limit theorem: if \(np_n\to r\), then for each \(k\) the binomial probability \(\binom nkp_n^k(1-p_n)^{n-k}\) tends to \(e^{-r}r^k/k!\) (`tendsto_choose_mul_pow_of_tendsto_mul_atTop`, `Probability/Distributions/Poisson/PoissonLimitThm.lean`).

#### TauCeti

With \(\mathbb P(M_n\le u)=F(u)^n\) from `TauCeti.Probability.cdf_max_iid` and \(a_n=-(1-F(u_n))\), Mathlib's limit gives \(\mathbb P(M_n\le u_n)\to e^{-\tau}\) when \(n(1-F(u_n))\to\tau<\infty\), and the Poisson limit theorem gives the pointwise limit of the binomial law of the number of exceedances. Neither consequence is stated, and the converse, the case \(\tau=\infty\), and the criterion for existence of the levels \(u_n\) are absent. That criterion requires both \(F(x)\to1\) and \((1-F(x))/(1-F(x-))\to1\) as \(x\uparrow x_F\). ## Missing from all three libraries

**Stationary processes and spectral theory.** The basic objects are absent: a weakly stationary process, its autocovariance, spectral distribution, and spectral density. TauCeti's Herglotz theorem supplies the spectral measure of a positive-definite sequence, so items 106.14 and 106.16 begin from there; the realization of a positive-definite function as the autocovariance of a Gaussian process needs Kolmogorov extension on \(\mathbb Z\), which TauCeti proves for countable index sets ([CKMA Structure 06](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE06.md)) and Mathlib for products of Polish spaces, together with a Gaussian projective family indexed by \(\mathbb Z\). The Wold decomposition (106.2) is a Hilbert-space statement about the shift on the closed span of the process; orthogonal projection and Gram–Schmidt are in Mathlib, but the Wold decomposition of an isometry is not. Kolmogorov's formula (106.16) needs the Szegő infimum theorem, which is absent ([CKMA Structure 42](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE42.md)). The Durbin–Levinson recursion (106.15) is finite-dimensional linear algebra on Toeplitz Gram matrices and is close to the existing projection and Gram-matrix material.

**Asymptotics of time-series estimators.** Items 106.1, 106.3–106.6, 106.12, 106.17, and 106.19 all require central limit theorems for dependent data: the martingale central limit theorem, or the linear-process central limit theorem, neither of which is present. Mathlib's i.i.d. central limit theorem, Lévy continuity, and Cramér–Wold are the starting point; a Lindeberg-type theorem for triangular arrays is the first missing step, and the conditional version for martingale differences is a further substantial step. The Fisher-information statements (106.5, 106.17) need a theory of Fisher information, which none of the three libraries defines. GARCH stationarity (106.18) needs a strong law for stationary ergodic sequences, that is, the pointwise ergodic theorem, which is absent; TauCeti's mean ergodic theorem gives only \(L^2\) convergence ([CKMA Structure 10](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE10.md)), and Mathlib's maximal ergodic theorem is the weak-type inequality from which the pointwise theorem is classically derived. Mixing coefficients and the block bootstrap (106.11), long-memory asymptotics (106.9), and likelihood inference for diffusions (106.10, which needs stochastic integration and Girsanov's theorem, absent per [CKMA Structure 08](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE08.md)) are further away.

**Filtering and state-space models.** The Kalman recursion (106.7) and the innovations likelihood (106.13) are within reach: TauCeti's Gaussian conditioning theorem is the one-step update, and an induction over \(t\) with the Gaussian affine-image and independence results of Mathlib would give the full recursion and the factorization. The hidden-Markov forward recursion (106.8) is a finite computation from the TauCeti Markov-chain path law together with a conditionally independent emission kernel; it is one construction away.

**Spatial statistics and point processes.** Simple and universal kriging (106.20, 106.23) are finite-dimensional least-squares statements, close to the existing projection and Schur-complement material. Stein's theorem (106.21) needs equivalence of Gaussian measures (Feldman–Hájek), which is absent ([CKMA Structure 09](../lean-CKMA/STRUCTURE09.md)). The Matérn family needs the modified Bessel function \(\mathcal K_\nu\), absent from Mathlib, after which positive definiteness follows from TauCeti's Bochner theorem and a Fourier-transform computation. Valid semivariograms (106.25) need the Lévy–Khintchine-type representation of conditionally negative definite functions (Schoenberg), not present. Poisson point processes, their likelihoods, and second-order summaries (106.22, 106.26, 106.27) need a construction of Poisson random measures, which none of the three libraries has; TauCeti's exponential renewal process gives Poisson counts on the half-line only.

**Survival analysis and counting processes.** Items 106.28–106.36 need counting processes, continuous-time compensators and martingales, stochastic integrals with respect to them, and weak convergence in the Skorokhod space; none of these exists. The finite-sample algebra of the Kaplan–Meier, Nelson–Aalen, and log-rank statistics could be formalized independently, and the observed-data density of item 106.31 is an elementary computation from the joint law of \((T,C)\), starting from TauCeti's law of a minimum, its hazard representation, and the additivity of hazards under independent minima; the limit theorems are far away.

**Sequential analysis.** Ville's inequality for nonnegative supermartingales (106.38) is one step from Mathlib's optional stopping and hitting times, and the confidence-sequence inversion (106.40) is then immediate. For the e-value part of 106.38, Statlib's e-variables and their independent products are the starting point; the conditional version, in which running products of conditional e-values form a supermartingale, is not formalized. Statlib proves Wald's identity for integrable stopping times of i.i.d. sequences and the likelihood ratio and Kullback–Leibler divergence of laws stopped at such times; what is missing for the sequential probability ratio test (106.37, 106.41) is the integrability of its exit time, the error bounds \(B\le(1-\beta)/\alpha\) and \(A\ge\beta/(1-\alpha)\), Wald's fundamental identity, and the Wald–Wolfowitz optimality, for which the stopped divergence identity supplies the information lower bound. Anscombe's theorem (106.42) and the Chow–Robbins rule (106.43) need random-index limit theorems; TauCeti's renewal strong law and central limit theorem are the nearest results. The CUSUM bridge limit (106.39) needs Donsker's theorem. Optimality of CUSUM and of the Shiryaev procedures (106.44, 106.45) needs optimal-stopping theory, which is absent.

**Extreme values.** The forward direction of the Poisson approximation (106.50) is a short assembly of existing TauCeti and Mathlib lemmas. TauCeti defines the limit families themselves, the generalized extreme-value and generalized Pareto laws with their max- and threshold-stability. Regular variation, the Fisher–Tippett–Gnedenko classification (106.46), threshold excesses (106.47), and the Hill estimator (106.49) need a theory of regularly varying functions, which none of the three libraries has; the convergence-of-types lemma is also absent. The extremal index (106.48) needs mixing conditions for dependent sequences. Likelihood for block maxima (106.51) needs general maximum-likelihood asymptotics for a nonregular parametric family, not present; TauCeti's density and quantile function of the generalized extreme-value law give the likelihood and the return level.
