# TauCeti coverage of Sections 101–108

This file summarises, section by section, what TauCeti adds to Mathlib for the
mathematical content described in [`SYNOPSIS101.md`](SYNOPSIS101.md)–[`SYNOPSIS108.md`](SYNOPSIS108.md).
It is a digest of the TauCeti parts of the coverage inventories
[`STRUCTURE101.md`](STRUCTURE101.md)–[`STRUCTURE108.md`](STRUCTURE108.md), which give the
declarations, source links, and hypotheses, and classify every numbered synopsis
entry as covered, partly covered, or not covered by Mathlib, TauCeti, and Statlib
together. The companion files [MathlibCoverage.md](MathlibCoverage.md) and
[StatlibCoverage.md](StatlibCoverage.md) summarise the Mathlib and Statlib
coverage in the same way, and [GAPS.md](GAPS.md) selects and assesses the principal
missing results. TauCeti and Statlib are developed independently on top of
Mathlib. Results that TauCeti imports from Mathlib are not attributed to
TauCeti here; where a TauCeti result specialises or repackages Mathlib material,
only the addition is described. Where Statlib proves the same result as
TauCeti, this is noted without preference.

TauCeti's contributions to this material are mostly distribution theory,
information theory, optimal transport and matrix analysis rather than
statistical theorems. Statistical inference proper is thinly covered. Five of
the eight verdicts below are Thin; the three Partial verdicts rest on the
entropic transport and Schrödinger material of 104C, on the Wishart and
Gaussian-projection distribution theory of 105A, and on the Wasserstein
barycenter, Fréchet mean, entropic transport and kernel embedding material of 108.

The classification of entries follows the uniform rule of
[STRUCTURE.md](STRUCTURE.md): an entry is partly covered when a library
declaration proves one of the claims the entry makes, or a special case of it;
an entry for which the libraries supply only ingredients, from which the
statement would follow by a further argument however short, is not covered.
Such *ingredients only* entries are mentioned below among the supporting
material, not as coverage.

These are direct-coverage verdicts under the CKSD rule. They are not
numerically comparable with CKMA's verdicts without reconciling the rules
and source revisions; see [Comparison with CKMA](STRUCTURE.md#comparison-with-ckma).

Each section below gives a verdict for this library alone, judged against the
content of the corresponding synopsis:

- **Substantial:** a large part of the graduate core of the section is proved.
- **Partial:** a real cluster of the section's own definitions and theorems is
  proved, but the section as a whole is not.
- **Thin:** a few of the section's results, or only neighbouring ingredients.
- **None:** nothing of the section's own content beyond general infrastructure.

## 101. Statistical inference: foundations, decision theory, estimation, and testing

**Thin.** TauCeti takes part in both covered entries of the section. With Mathlib it completes the relative-entropy entry 101.44: the divergence tensorizes over products, and the chain rule is identified with the averaged divergence of the conditional kernels (Statlib proves the product and averaged chain rules as well). Its Pinsker inequality, proved without absolute continuity, completes 101.66 together with Statlib's testing identity. The distribution library defines the chi-squared, Student \(t\) and Fisher \(F\) laws and proves the known-mean chi-squared law of a Gaussian sum of squares, the \(t\) and \(F\) ratio constructions, and the chi-squared laws and independence of orthogonal projections of a standard Gaussian (101.17, partly, with Mathlib's normality of \(\bar X\); the identification of \((n-1)S^2/\sigma^2\) with such a projection, hence its law and its independence from \(\bar X\), is absent). For an i.i.d. sample from any real law, atoms included, the \(k\)-th order statistic has the binomial-tail distribution function, for uniform samples it is beta-distributed, and the sorted tuple of an atomless sample has \(n!\) times the product law on the increasing chamber (101.21, partly; the marginal density for general \(F\) and the law of the ranks are absent). The second moment of a Euclidean law as squared mean plus covariance trace is the bias–variance decomposition (101.27, partly, with Mathlib), and Stein's identity is proved in dimension one for everywhere-differentiable functions (101.64, partly). Supporting are one-parameter tilting with closure of the Gaussian, gamma, Poisson and binomial families, moment-generating-function determinacy near \(0\) (the analytic step of completeness), the probability integral transform (101.25 has ingredients only), the binomial tail, beta quantiles and stochastic order of binomial laws (the unstated Clopper–Pearson bound), the hypergeometric and beta-binomial laws, gamma laws of exponential sums (101.28 has ingredients only), the noncentral \(t\) law (101.56 has ingredients only), the \(F\) law with the moment-generating function of Gaussian quadratic forms (101.60 has ingredients only), the law of total variance for mixtures, convexity of the divergence, Csiszár's Pythagorean identity and the Donsker–Varadhan formula. Nothing is added on sufficiency, Fisher information, unbiased estimation, tests and confidence sets, decision theory, admissibility or equivariance. [Details in STRUCTURE101.md](STRUCTURE101.md).

## 102. Asymptotic, semiparametric, and robust statistics

**Thin.** TauCeti's one asymptotic statistical theorem is the strong consistency of empirical Fréchet barycenters: for i.i.d. points of a separable pseudometric space with finite \(p\)-th moment, almost surely every cluster point of empirical \(p\)-barycenters is a population barycenter, the minimal empirical radius converges, and on a proper space the barycenters converge when the population barycenter is unique. The proof goes through almost-sure \(W_p\) convergence of empirical measures. This is a particular \(M\)-estimator (102.23, partly; the general criterion is absent). The remaining material is supporting: the quantile function with its Galois characterization and the exact laws of order statistics, the clipped law behind the trimmed mean, the chi-squared law of Gaussian sums of squares (102.27 has ingredients only) and of Gaussian projections, the moment-generating function of Gaussian quadratic forms, the noncentral chi-squared law of \(\|X\|^2\) for \(X\sim N(m,I_k)\), affine images of multivariate Gaussians (the covariance \(Dg\,\Sigma\,Dg^{\mathsf T}\) of the delta method), the randomization coding of kernels, Gaussian tilting (the Gaussian side of Le Cam's third lemma in dimension one), a pointwise Hellinger–KL integrand inequality, Gaussian distribution functions and quantiles, the multinomial covariance behind Pearson's theorem, the Hermite basis of \(L^2(\gamma)\), Fourier inversion and lattice frequencies of characteristic functions, the distribution-function and bounded-Lipschitz characterizations of weak convergence, Lindeberg's replacement inequality, and the sampling-with-replacement law, which is exactly the bootstrap resampling law given the sample. No limit theorem of the section is proved: nothing is added on the delta method, maximum likelihood, contiguity and local asymptotic normality, U-statistics, semiparametric efficiency, influence functions and robust estimation, higher-order expansions, or bootstrap consistency. [Details in STRUCTURE102.md](STRUCTURE102.md).

## 103. Nonparametric and high-dimensional inference

**Thin.** TauCeti reaches two entries. For a standard Gaussian vector and a linear subspace \(K\), \(\|P_KY\|^2\) has the \(\chi^2_{\dim K}\) law, which is the null law of the cone test when the cone is a subspace (103.12, partly; general polyhedral cones and the likelihood-ratio identity are absent). Ky Fan's trace inequality bounds the variance captured by a rank-\(k\) orthogonal projection by the sum of the \(k\) largest eigenvalues, with attainment by some orthonormal family for positive operators, which is the finite-dimensional deterministic core of the optimality of principal components (103.31, partly; attainment by the leading eigenvectors, uniqueness and the Hilbert-space case are not stated). Supporting material is substantial but stops short of statements: the probability integral transform, inverse transform sampling and the joint law of order statistics of an atomless sample, \(L^p\) approximate identities (the bias half of \(L^1\) consistency of kernel density estimates), the Poisson-binomial law of counts of independent events (103.18 has ingredients only), the Prékopa–Leindler and Brunn–Minkowski inequalities with log-concavity of marginals, a Hilbert eigenbasis for compact symmetric operators (the deterministic Karhunen–Loève step), kernel mean embeddings, Gaussian projection laws (103.36 has ingredients only), the Marchenko–Pastur law and the Gaussian ensembles (103.38 has ingredients only), a lower bound for Gaussian maxima, Gaussian concentration, Bernstein's inequality and sub-exponential sums, an operator-norm tail bound for sub-Gaussian matrices, Gaussian conditioning, Pinsker's inequality and tensorization of the divergence (103.39 and 103.46 have ingredients only, with Statlib's testing bounds), the identity of `etvDist` with twice the supremum over sets (also proved in Statlib), Hamming minimum distance and covering numbers, Schatten and Ky Fan norms, sample second-moment matrices with polynomial deviation bounds, Wishart laws, and the dimension-free Davis–Kahan \(\sin\Theta\) theorem with Weyl's bound and the Yu–Wang–Samworth eigenvector bound (103.54 has ingredients only). ## 104. Bayesian inference and stochastic computation

**Partial.** The verdict rests on the entropic side of 104C; the rest of the section is thin. TauCeti defines the static and dynamic Schrödinger problems, proves existence and uniqueness of the minimizer, the reduction of the dynamic to the static problem, and the endpoint-density form of the bridge property (104.66, partly; the Brownian reference is absent). It proves the Pythagorean identity, minimality and uniqueness of potentials for couplings of product form (104.71, partly), the continuity equation for the time marginals of laws on curves and of plan interpolations, with the conditional-expectation velocity (104.67, partly), and the upper action bound, equality with \(W_2^2\) for optimal couplings, and the Benamou–Brenier lower bound (104.72, partly; the left inequality for the straight interpolant of a general coupling is not stated). Relative-entropy projection onto a prescribed marginal gives the half-bridges, and for iterative proportional fitting the telescoping identity, monotonicity and convergence of the marginal errors (104.77, partly; path-space iteration and convergence of couplings are absent). The JKO step has minimizers under abstract hypotheses, unique under generalized-geodesic convexity, with lower semicontinuity and coercivity of the Boltzmann entropy (104.43, partly). Inverse transform sampling and the probability integral transform are covered (104.28). Gaussian conditioning gives the known-variance normal posterior for one observation (104.4, partly), a Gamma–Gaussian factorization gives the conjugate posterior of a precision after one observation (104.2, partly), and TauCeti adds the sample-mean variance to 104.21 and the evidence identity for nonnegative potentials to 104.25 (both partly, with Mathlib). Supporting are the Beta, Gamma, Dirichlet and multinomial families with the beta-binomial and Gamma–Poisson marginals (104.7 has ingredients only), Dirichlet aggregation and one step of stick breaking (104.15 has ingredients only), meeting-time coupling, periods and recurrence of Markov chains, EVI contraction, convexity of potential energy, McCann's displacement convexity of internal energies (104.38 has ingredients only), minimizing movements, Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities as definitions (104.44 has ingredients only), Girsanov's theorem for a constant drift, Pinsker's inequality (104.68 has ingredients only), Gaussian integration by parts and multinomial moments (104.61 has ingredients only). Nothing is added on general conjugate updates, posterior asymptotics, the Dirichlet process, convergence of Markov-chain samplers, Langevin dynamics and the Fokker–Planck equation, particle methods or score-based diffusion models. ## 105. Linear models, multivariate analysis, experimental design, and causal inference

**Partial.** The verdict rests on the multivariate normal distribution theory of 105A, where seven entries are reached in part and the Wishart and Gaussian-projection results form a connected cluster, although the sampling theorems built on them are absent. The Gram sum of \(n\ge p\) independent \(N_p(0,\Sigma)\) vectors has the nonsingular Wishart density law (105.22, partly; \(\bar X\), \(S\) and Hotelling's \(T^2\) are absent), and the Wishart density, congruence, principal-submatrix marginals, the Bartlett decomposition, the determinant law and the Schur-complement law for a block of size one are proved (105.31, partly; general block size and the independence statement are absent). For a standard Gaussian vector, \(\|PY\|^2\) and \(\|(I-P)Y\|^2\) are independent chi-squared variables (105.2, partly; general mean, scaling and the least-squares estimator are absent). Independence of Gaussian blocks is equivalent to vanishing cross-covariance (105.29, partly), and Ky Fan's inequality with attainment gives the maximal captured variance \(\lambda_1+\dots+\lambda_k\) (105.30, partly; the leading eigenvectors and the Frobenius Eckart–Young theorem are not stated). Gaussian conditioning with affine images gives the conditional mean of random effects in the mixed model with \(\beta\) known (105.14, partly), and the first trigonometric moment of the von Mises law gives \(\mathbb E\cos(X-\mu)=I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)\) (105.27, partly). Supporting are the chi-squared law and its convolution, the moment-generating function of Gaussian quadratic forms with the independence criterion for linear images (105.15 has ingredients only), the noncentral chi-squared and \(F\) laws, the \(t\) and \(F\) ratio laws, the trace of an idempotent as its rank, the covariance matrix and Gaussian density, finite multivariate Gaussian mixture laws and densities with their moments (105.6 has ingredients only), Courant–Fischer, operator-norm Eckart–Young, the singular-value Hoffman–Wielandt inequality, principal cosines, the Gamma–Beta relation, Wishart moments and transforms, the von Mises–Fisher law, weak union for conditional independence, concavity of \((\det A)^{1/n}\) (the D-criterion), finite iterative proportional fitting, sampling without replacement (105.72 and 105.73 have ingredients only), the functional representation of conditional independence (105.61 has ingredients only), the modified Bessel function \(I_\nu\), and the logistic derivative. ## 107. Statistical learning and machine learning

## 108. Geometry of data: manifolds, topology, optimal transport, and kernels

**Partial.** TauCeti supplies all of the section's coverage beyond the graph Laplacian. Wasserstein barycenters exist on proper spaces and are unique in \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^d)\) when one law is absolutely continuous (108.41, covered), and empirical Wasserstein barycenters of i.i.d. random laws on a proper Polish space are consistent (108.49, covered). On the line the barycenter has the averaged quantile function, the quantile map is an isometry onto the nondecreasing classes, \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)\) is CAT(0), and population barycenters exist and are unique (108.45, partly); the Bures formula with a positive-definite source and the Gaussian barycenter of finitely many laws given a positive-definite solution of the fixed-point equation are proved (108.46, partly). Fréchet barycenters on metric spaces have compact mean sets and almost-sure consistency (108.14, partly), with uniqueness in CAT(0) spaces and existence and consistency in proper CAT(0) spaces (108.5, partly). Ky Fan's principle gives the optimal orthogonal Procrustes rotation over \(O(m)\) (108.16, partly). The Sinkhorn divergence is nonnegative, and vanishes exactly on the diagonal for universal Gibbs kernels, for bounded nonnegative costs on compact spaces (108.44, partly). Entropic duality with attainment, complementary slackness and Sinkhorn half-steps (108.54, partly), the Wasserstein tangent space and the linearization bound (108.57, partly), the kernel mean embedding with the double-integral formula for \(\mathrm{MMD}^2\) (108.60, partly), the characteristic property of universal kernels on compact metrizable spaces (108.68, partly), and weak convergence implied by \(W_p\) convergence (108.69, partly) complete the list. Supporting are Riemannian exponential maps and Hopf–Rinow, Courant–Fischer, operator-norm Eckart–Young, \(\sin\Theta\) bounds, Gabriel's theorem, the Čech–singular comparison, simplicial chains with Euler–Poincaré identities (108.31 has ingredients only), the Morse lemma, almost-sure empirical \(W_p\) convergence without rates, the Gromov–Wasserstein pseudodistance and its metric quotient (108.43 has ingredients only), stability of transport problems, quantization errors, the convolution contraction of \(W_p\), Brenier's theorem, entropic barycenters, linear rates for discrete Sinkhorn iterations, the Wasserstein subdifferential, Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality, Bochner's theorem with the Gaussian kernel, McDiarmid's inequality and covering numbers. Nothing is added on spectral embeddings, persistent homology and its stability and inference, statistical rates of transport, transport-map estimation, MMD tests, kernel Stein discrepancies or random features.
